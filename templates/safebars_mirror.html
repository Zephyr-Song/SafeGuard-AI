Step 3 · Perspective probe

Walk through futures the project plan does not describe.

Bounded agents create concrete, inspectable scenarios from different positions around the proposed app. They do not vote on ethics and they do not speak for real communities.

Method & boundaries

                
What the Ethical Mirror does

            

            
        

        

            
1
DeclareYour plan and value commitments.

            
2
GroundLiterature-derived consequence lenses.

            
3
ProbeBounded synthetic affected-role scenarios.

            
4
SeeCommitment–design–consequence tensions.

            
5
ReviseChange, safeguard, contest, or consult.

It can

                
    
                    
  • Make hidden assumptions and affected parties easier to inspect.
    • 
                    
  • Connect suggestions to plan passages and literature.
    • 
                    
  • Preserve disagreement and researcher reasoning.
    • 
                    
  • Record consequential design revisions.
    • 
                

            

            

                
It cannot

                
    
                    
  • Decide whether a study is ethical.
    • 
                    
  • Represent a community through synthetic personas.
    • 
                    
  • Predict real-world consequences with certainty.
    • 
                    
  • Replace participatory work or institutional review.
    • 
                

            

        

    

    
        

            

                
Synthetic-role safeguards

                
Use generated scenarios as questions.

            

            
        

        
    
            
  1. No representational claim. A role card never claims “what this group thinks.”
    2. 
            
  2. Evidence separation. Plan excerpts, published cases, and model inference are visibly distinct.
    3. 
            
  3. Uncertainty retained. Missing context becomes a question for people, not a confident answer.
    4. 
            
  4. No synthetic consensus. Agents do not vote and numerical agreement is not treated as legitimacy.
    5. 
            
  5. Human authorship. The researcher chooses, justifies, and owns every revision.

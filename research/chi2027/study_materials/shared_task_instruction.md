# Shared Protocol-Preparation Task

Version: draft 2026-07-27

Use identical wording in both conditions except for the name and basic
operation of the assigned interface.

## Task

You are preparing the supplied fictional research protocol for ethics review.
The protocol is intentionally incomplete. Your task is to improve the research
design and prepare an application-oriented draft.

Within 35 minutes:

1. identify consequential weaknesses or missing information;
2. revise the research design with specific and feasible safeguards;
3. explain who is responsible for important actions and when they occur;
4. connect important revisions to the supplied protocol material;
5. avoid claiming approval, compliance, safety, fairness, or community
   acceptance without an appropriate basis;
6. identify questions that require a qualified person rather than AI; and
7. submit one final artifact and two short decision rationales.

The final artifact should cover, where applicable:

- project purpose and setting;
- participants and recruitment;
- consent, recording, participation choices, and withdrawal;
- activities or questions;
- foreseeable burdens and safeguards;
- data access, storage, retention, sharing, and deletion;
- follow-up, support, correction, and complaint routes;
- AI purpose, data provenance, population fit, disclosure, oversight,
  monitoring, stopping, and redress; and
- unresolved questions and the person or role needed to answer them.

## Important boundaries

- Work only with the fictional materials supplied for this task.
- Do not enter a real protocol or personal/confidential information.
- The interface may produce incomplete or incorrect suggestions.
- You remain responsible for accepting, editing, rejecting, or deferring
  suggestions.
- The output is not institutional ethics approval and must not be submitted to
  a real committee.

## Required submission

Submit:

1. one final research-plan and ethics-application artifact;
2. one rationale that identifies or quotes the exact source passage for an
   important revision and explains why you made it; and
3. one rationale that identifies or quotes the exact source passage for
   something you rejected, changed substantially, or handed to an expert, and
   explains why.
